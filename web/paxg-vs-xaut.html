PAXG vs XAUT: how the two largest tokenized gold tokens differ

Both tokens claim one troy ounce of gold per token. That sentence is where the similarity ends. The differences that matter to a holder are custody, redemption, chains and fees — and they are all verifiable from the issuers' own disclosures.

2. What actually differs

Custody and issuer risk

A tokenized gold position is a claim on a custodian, not a bar in your hand. Two different issuers means two different legal entities, vault arrangements and attestation practices. If you hold both, you are diversifying issuer risk — which is the main reason to hold both at all.

Redemption path

Both issuers describe a redemption process, but it runs through them: minimum sizes, KYC, fees, and jurisdiction restrictions. For most holders the token is traded, not redeemed. If physical delivery matters to you, read the redemption terms first — they are the part most comparisons skip.

Chain and liquidity

Liquidity lives where market makers are. PAXG's market is concentrated on Ethereum; XAUT also has a large Tron presence. If you plan to move size, check the venues you would actually use, not a static table: depth and spreads change weekly.

Fees

Issuance and redemption fees are issuer-specific and can change. On-chain trading fees depend on the venue and the chain, not on the token. A comparison that quotes one number for "the fee" is out of date by the time it is published.

3. What neither token does

  • No yield. Gold does not pay interest, and neither token does. Any yield product is a separate credit exposure.
  • No guaranteed peg. The market price can trade above or below the issuer's reference value, especially on thin venues.
  • No physical delivery by default. Delivery is a redemption process with conditions, not a feature of the token.

5. A short checklist before you buy

  • Read the issuer's current redemption terms (minimums, KYC, fees, jurisdiction).
  • Check the latest attestation date, not just its existence.
  • Check the venue's depth for the size you plan to trade.
  • Decide whether you are diversifying issuer risk or just chasing a spread.
