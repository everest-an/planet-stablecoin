Planet — an on-chain commodity purchasing-power index.

Planet (PLT) aggregates the real prices of a basket of global commodities into a single on-chain purchasing-power index (PPI), expressed as a TEP-74 token on TON. Read the light summary, or the full version with the complete mathematics.

1. Overview

Planet (PLT) aggregates the real prices of a basket of global commodities — energy, metals and agriculture — into an on-chain purchasing-power index (PPI), and expresses that index as a TEP-74 token on TON. A wallet can mint PLT by depositing value at the current PPI, and burn PLT to redeem against the basket.

Phase 1 status. Planet is a proof-of-concept synthetic index token. It holds no real commodity reserve and is not a fully-collateralised stablecoin. The full “1:1 commodity reserve” variant is a future phase that requires an RWA custodian and regulatory compliance.

3. The index (PPI)

The owner configures a weighted basket — currently 14 commodities (WTI 14% · Brent 11% · natural gas 8% · gold 12% · silver 7% · copper 6% · wheat 9% · corn 7% · soybean 6% · coffee 4% · lumber 4% · lithium 3% · water 5% · carbon 4%) — and locks the base prices on-chain. A relayer submits signed per-asset prices; PlanetCore recomputes the PPI from the live basket. An asset flagged as frozen is excluded and the remaining weights are re-normalised.

4. Mint / burn and fees

Minting deposits value and issues PLT at the current PPI, net of a tiered fee. Each fee is routed 30% burn · 50% reserve · 20% staking, less a 10% risk-fund carve-out. Burning goes through the TEP-74 jetton wallet and is mirrored back to core accounting so supply stays consistent.

5. Stability mechanisms

  • Band circuit-breaker: minting is refused when the index is undefined, and blocked beyond a 15% deviation.
  • Fee slope: fees rise above a 5% deviation; redemption is asymmetric and capped.
  • Arbitrage incentive: a fee rebate when the index is rich, to pull it back.
  • Stabilisation bonds & risk fund: absorb selling pressure below peg; part of each fee funds a risk reserve.

6. Oracle

Prices are sourced off-chain from two independent feeds, combined as a median, and signed with ed25519. PlanetCore verifies against an M-of-N signer set (distinct signers enforced) and applies guards: strictly increasing timestamps, a single-update price-jump limit, a freshness ceiling on minting, and automatic freeze on source divergence.

7. Governance

All privileged actions require the owner. Ownership is migrated to a timelock and routed through Propose → delay → Execute, giving holders a reaction window; the timelock’s owner should be a multisig for M-of-N plus delay. A staking token (PLTG) distributes fee revenue pro-rata and carries stake-weighted parameter signalling. See §9 of the full version for the original DAO plan and exactly what is live today.

8. Status & roadmap

  • Now: TON testnet; contracts implemented and tested (239 tests); multi-source oracle live; key separation done.
  • Next: third-party audit; multisig owner; production oracle sources; the full PLTG DAO.
  • Later: 1:1 commodity reserve via an RWA custodian, proof-of-reserve and compliance.

9. Disclaimer

This is a technical proposal and a proof-of-concept, not investment advice. It is not audited. Commodity prices are volatile and an early-stage index token may diverge materially from its theoretical PPI. Do not use with value you cannot afford to lose.

1. Problem & design goals

  • Single commodities (gold, oil) are volatile and concentrated; a multi-commodity exposure is hard to hold on-chain.
  • Inflation hedging exists, but there is no public, verifiable object equal to “one token = a basket of global commodity purchasing power”.
  • Design goals: (i) a transparent index whose formula is on-chain; (ii) a token that is redeemable through the commodity layer; (iii) failure isolation, so one bad feed cannot poison the whole index; (iv) no custodian in Phase 1; (v) every parameter change observable and delayed.

Settlement layer: TON. All arithmetic is integer fixed-point (1e6 scale for the index; 1e12 for reward-per-share), because the TVM has no floats.

3. The index: PPI

Let the basket be assets i = 1…n with weight wi, base price Pibase (locked) and current price Pi. Let A be the set of active (non-frozen) assets.

(1)PPI = Σi∈A wi · PiPibaseΣi∈A wi (2)PPI = 0  if  Σi∈A wi = 0  (no active asset ⇒ the index is undefined)
  • Weights: Σi wi = 106 (= 100%) and 0 ≤ wi ≤ 150000 (= 15%). Enforced on every price update.
  • Re-normalisation: dividing by the active weight sum is what makes a frozen asset disappear cleanly instead of silently biasing the index.
  • Equation (2) matters: an earlier implementation returned 1.0 when everything was frozen, which let a caller mint against a fabricated index. It now returns 0 and minting is refused (§4).
  • Target price: PricePLT = PPI × USDbase, with USDbase = $1.00 at genesis — i.e. PLT tracks the ratio of today's basket price to the locked base, not a fixed dollar.

4. Mint, redeem and fees

Let D be the value deposited, B the PLT burned, and f the fee rate for the current band (below).

(3)M = D · (1 − f) / PPI   PLT minted (4)R = B · PPI · (1 − fburn)   value returned (5)F = D · f   fee collected

Minting is gated by two independent conditions:

(6)tnow − tlastUpdate ≤ MAX_ORACLE_AGE = 6 h   freshness (7)PPI > 0   the index is defined (§3 eq. 2)

(6) means the protocol never mints while the oracle is stale; (7) closes the frozen-basket hole. Together they are the only reason a mint can be refused for “market” reasons.

Fee routing

(8)F = Fburn + Freserve + Fstake + Frisk burn 30%  ·  reserve 50%  ·  staking 20%, then 10% of the fee is carved out into the risk fund (net 30 / 40 / 20 / 10)

The reserve bucket is the largest deliberately: in the fully-backed future phase the reserve must grow at least as fast as the index, otherwise the collateral ratio decays.

5.2 Asymmetry and arbitrage

(11)fmint′ = fmint − arbRebateBps  when PPI > 1.03

Redemption is deliberately not symmetric with minting: exiting below peg carries an extra penalty (bounded by (10)), while minting below peg is what re-floats the index. When the index is rich the protocol pays a rebate so an external arbitrageur is rewarded for pushing price back toward PPI.

5.3 Risk fund and bonds

(12)Frisk += F · RISK_SHARE   funded on every fee (13)reserve += a  if  riskFund ≥ a   owner CoverDeficit (14)face = principal · (1 + premiumBps/104)   bond

Bonds are requested by the holder and settled by the operator — asking to redeem can never destroy a claim before it has been paid. Per-transaction limits (SetLimits) are the hard circuit-breaker.

6. CAM adapter layer — failure isolation

Each commodity has a CAM (Commodity Adapter Module) emitting a standardised tuple (price, volatility, liquidity, riskState) with riskState ∈ {normal, caution, frozen}.

(15)frozen ⇒ i ∉ A  ⇒ excluded from (1) and exempt from rebalancing

This is the core safety property: a single bad source, price or liquidity event is contained. The protocol propagates the state (raising fees / lowering limits) rather than the error, so the index cannot be poisoned by one commodity.

7. Oracle & price integrity

The relayer reads two independent sources per commodity, takes the median, and signs a payload with an ed25519 key. Anyone may relay it; authenticity comes from the signature. Let H be the payload hash and S the signer set:

(16)accept ⇔ |{ s ∈ S : verify(H, σs, pubs) }| ≥ threshold  distinct signers (bitmask) (17)tpayload > tlastUpdate  strictly increasing ⇒ replay is dead (18)|Pnew − Pold| / Pold ≤ 50%  single-update jump limit (19)spread(sources) / median > 2% ⇒ freeze  divergence guard

Domain separation (PRICE_DOMAIN) prevents cross-deployment or cross-message replay. Keys live in a set: writing slot i revokes whatever was there.

7.1 Freshness model (two different clocks)

ClockValueMeaning
On-chain mint guard6 hminting is refused on stale prices (safety, eq. 6)
API/monitor health SLA26 hwhen the feed is reported UNHEALTHY — i.e. “the scheduled push actually missed”

They are intentionally different: the first is a safety property, the second is a monitoring threshold matched to the daily push cadence. Consequence, stated plainly: with a daily push, minting is only available within 6 h of a push.

8. Rebalancing (RTRA)

(20)ΔAi = ( wi − Vi / Σj Vj ) · T
  • ΔAi — value to trade in asset i; wi — target weight; Vi — current holding; T — total reserve.
  • Trigger: drift-based, and risk-adaptive — a frozen asset is not traded, and high volatility / low liquidity raises the trigger threshold.
  • Execution is deliberately out of scope: Planet does not build a DEX/AMM/order-book. Trades happen on external venues.

9. Governance & the DAO plan

9.1 What the original whitepaper (v1.0) planned

The first whitepaper (v1.0, Solana era) specified a governance token PLTG, fixed at 1,000,000,000 units, with a published allocation and vesting schedule:

BucketShareVesting
Community incentives40%5-year linear (liquidity mining)
Protocol treasury25%DAO-controlled, ecosystem
Core team15%2-year lock + 3-year linear
Investors12%1-year lock + 2-year linear
Public sale8%fully released at TGE

The DAO was to control, by PLTG vote:

  • commodity weights wi — “adjusted quarterly by DAO vote”;
  • fee rates (stated range 0.3%–0.8%);
  • the PPI deviation thresholds (±3% default);
  • the choice of reserve custodian.

PLTG was to capture value two ways: staking dividends — a share of fees paid periodically in USDC — and voting power. Liquidity providers of the PLT/USDC pool were to receive PLTG rewards. The roadmap scheduled DAO governance launch in Q2 2026. Anti-fragility commitments included spreading reserve assets across 10+ venues and a minimum collateral ratio of 105%.

Note the v1 fee split was 45 / 30 / 25 (buyback-burn / reserve / staking); the implemented split is now 30 / 50 (+10 risk) / 20, i.e. the reserve bucket was re-weighted upward. This is a deliberate change, not an oversight.

Honest summary. The economic layer of the DAO (PLTG staking and fee dividends) is implemented and tested. The decision layer is not: votes are advisory, and real parameter authority sits with the owner behind a 60-second timelock on testnet. Closing that gap — binding, executed proposals — is a deliberate design step, not a claim we make today.

9.3 Privileged actions and the timelock

(21)Execute possible ⇔ now ≥ eta = proposeTime + delay  ∧  ∃ a pending (dest, body)

Owner-gated operations: basket weights / add-asset, fee parameters, limits, oracle signer set, pause, and ownership itself. Ownership changes must themselves go through Propose → delay → Execute; a direct owner call is rejected. The delay gives holders a reaction window, and the timelock’s owner should be a multisig (M-of-N plus delay).

9.3.1 Staking dividend mathematics

(22)accRPS ← accRPS + ⌊ R · 1012 / S ⌋   R = reward, S = total staked (23)claimable = ⌊ si · accRPS / 1012 ⌋ − debti (24)debti ← ⌊ si · accRPS / 1012 ⌋   on every stake/unstake/claim

A classic reward-per-share accumulator. Note (22) floors: dust stays in the pool rather than being minted. Reconciling that dust is one of the things an auditor should check.

11. Trust & threat model

ActorCanCannot
Usermint / redeem / stake / vote / transfer — always signing in their own wallet—
Hot signing keysubmit price payloads (bounded by eq. 16–19)touch ownership, parameters or funds
Ownerchange parameters after the timelock delayact instantly; bypass Propose → delay → Execute
  • Owner-key compromise is the dominant risk; the delay is the only mitigation — hence multisig + a longer delay before mainnet.
  • Hot-key compromise is bounded: the attacker can only push prices inside the jump band and divergence freeze, and can never move funds.
  • Payload replay is defeated by the strictly increasing timestamp (eq. 17).
  • By design, not fixed: the redemption mirror has no freshness guard (asymmetric with minting), the burn bucket is accounting-only, and proposals do not execute. These are documented, not hidden.
