What is Planet?

Planet is an on-chain commodity purchasing-power index on Arbitrum Sepolia. It reads the real prices of a basket of 14 global commodities — energy, metals, agriculture and industrial materials — combines them into one number (the PPI), and expresses that number as a ERC-20 token (PLT). It is a data product first and a token second.

Is PLT a stablecoin?

No. PLT is a synthetic index token: it tracks the index, it is not redeemable for commodities or dollars, and no reserve backs it. There is no peg and no promise of one. The Phase 1 build is a proof of concept on testnet; anything describing PLT as a stablecoin or a yield product is wrong.

What is the PPI?

PPI stands for purchasing-power index: a single number that tracks how the price of the whole basket moves, where 1.000000 is the index value at launch (the base date). If the PPI reads 1.012, the basket has risen about 1.2% since launch. It is quoted with six decimals and computed on-chain.

How is the index calculated?

Each commodity contributes its weight times the ratio of its current price to its base price. The weighted contributions are summed and divided by the sum of the active weights. A commodity whose feed is flagged as frozen is excluded and the remaining weights are renormalised, so a single bad data source cannot corrupt the whole index.

What is in the basket, and who sets the weights?

The live basket holds 14 commodities — WTI and Brent crude, natural gas, gold, silver, copper, wheat, corn, soybean, coffee, and lumber, lithium, water and carbon proxies. No single commodity may exceed 15% and the weights must sum to 100%. Weights are set by governance; base prices are frozen once the basket is locked.

Where do the prices come from?

An off-chain relayer fetches each commodity from two independent sources (for example Yahoo Finance and Sina), takes the median, signs the result with an ed25519 key and pushes it to the contract, which verifies the signature on-chain. If the two sources diverge by more than 2%, that commodity is marked frozen and excluded.

How often are prices updated?

A scheduled run pushes a commodity only when its price has moved more than 0.1% since the last on-chain value, refreshed by a daily scheduled run; the on-chain index is recomputed on every update. On a quiet day that means a handful of transactions instead of fourteen — cheap and still fresh.

How do I mint or redeem PLT?

Open the Mini App at app.ppppp.space and connect a wallet. There are two paths: /app mints and redeems against the Arbitrum contracts from an EVM wallet (note: mint() takes a declared deposit and transfers no collateral — it is a testnet accounting exercise), and the Telegram Mini App does the same on TON. You always sign in your own wallet — Planet never holds keys.

What fees are there?

The core charges a band-based fee of 0.5% near the index, 1% when the index is 3–5% off, and 2% beyond that, with an extra penalty for exiting far below. Fees are split 30% buyback-burn, 40% reserve, 20% staking and 10% risk fund. On testnet the numbers are real but the assets are not.

Is there a governance token?

Yes — PLTG, a fixed-supply (1,000,000,000) TON-side token (not deployed on Arbitrum) with a live staking and voting contract. Stakers receive the staking share of protocol fees pro-rata, and voting power is weighted by the amount staked. It is deployed on testnet and not for sale.

Which network is it on? Is it live on mainnet?

Two testnets, no mainnet. The Arbitrum Sepolia deployment holds the basket, the on-chain index and the PLT token, and is what this site reads. The TON testnet build is still live and is what the Mini App's mint/redeem talks to. There is no public sale, no exchange listing, and the published addresses are testnet addresses whose tokens carry no value. Mainnet is gated behind an independent audit.

Is the code audited?

Not by a third party yet — that is the main gate before any mainnet launch. The code is open, written in Solidity (Arbitrum) and Tolk (TON), and backed by a continuous test suite (200+ tests, coverage gate at 90%), plus a self-review of known failure classes. Independent audit is planned, not done; treat this as unaudited software.

Who controls the basket weights?

Governance does, through two on-chain actions: adding a commodity, or re-weighting an existing one (including setting a weight to zero to retire it). Base prices can never be changed, so the index history stays tamper-evident. On testnet the owner is a 60-second timelock; production would use a multisig behind a longer delay.

What are the main risks?

Four matter. The index can diverge from any real-market value because it is a synthetic construction. Oracle data can be wrong or manipulated, which is why there are two sources, a jump guard and a freeze mechanism. The code is unaudited. And liquidity is thin, so any secondary price can move sharply. This is a research prototype, not a savings product.

How do I verify it myself?

Read the contracts directly. The core exposes ppi(), assetCount(), assetAt(i) and lastOracleUpdate(); the live basket is rendered at /dashboard, and the same data is available as JSON from /api/v1/status. Everything is public and reproducible — you do not have to trust this page.
